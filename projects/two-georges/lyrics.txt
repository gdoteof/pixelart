[Intro - Town Crier: booming announcer]
Hear ye, hear ye! Three thousand miles of sea between 'em!
In the East, the King. In the West, the General.
Two Georges. One continent. Let's go!

[Verse 1 - King George III: haughty, posh British accent, sneering]
Kneel, colonial. The Crown would like a word:
you're George the First of Nothing. I'm George the Third.
You didn't make your fortune, boy, you married it.
Martha had the acres. You were just tall enough to carry it.
Twenty-two, one volley in the woods lit war across my empire.
Father of your country? Please. You're the father of a fire.
Then it poured on Fort Necessity, your powder soaking wet.
That's the closest thing to a reign you're ever gonna get.
You marched out on the Fourth of July. Look it up, boy:
your first Independence Day? A white flag. Enjoy.
I paid for the war you started, sent a Stamp, you called it poison.
That's not tyranny, colonial. That's an invoice.
You begged to wear my redcoat. Wrote letters, pled and pined.
Loudoun left you on read. Commission: declined.
You're not a rebel, George. You're a reject with a gun.
I'm the Crown. You're the help that got ideas. Kneel. I'm done.

[Verse 2 - George Washington: calm, cold, deep American voice]
You done?
Four balls through my coat on the Monongahela,
two horses shot from under me, and I'm still here to tell ya:
Farmer George, you spread manure, but you can't make me sweat.
You're all powder and no ball. I haven't felt it yet.
Sure, I lost the Fourth of July in fifty-four. It's true.
Now the whole world spends the Fourth celebrating losing you.
"Left on read"? We sent the Olive Branch, on our knees, to plead.
You wouldn't even take it. You left a whole continent on read.
You rented Hessians. I crossed the ice and hit 'em at eight a.m.
Boxing Day in Trenton? I boxed up nine hundred of 'em.
You never came to fight yourself, so we melted down your statue:
forty-two thousand bullets, George. We fired you back at you.
You wanted quarters in our houses? Here's a quarter from my hand.
That's my face on it, George. I'm the currency of this land.

[Verse 3 - King George III: haughty, posh British accent, rattled and vicious]
"Currency"? Your money crashed so hard it's a phrase:
"not worth a Continental." My forgers printed it for days.
And your record, General? The fort that had your name on it
surrendered twenty-eight hundred men by teatime. Own it.
Your favorite, Benedict Arnold, flipped his coat to red.
Your best general saw us both and picked the other George instead.
The cherry tree? A parson made it up to sell a book.
The one story of you not lying? It's a lie. Go look.
I reigned for sixty years. You did eight, then ran home to the plow.
Couldn't hold a crown, so you called quitting "noble." Take a bow.
And spare me "liberty." Your man Harry ran to me and sailed off free,
while you begged to get back your "property."
Your teeth ain't wood, that's folklore. It's hippo ivory beneath,
and nine you bought from people that you owned,
so every time you said "freedom," George, you said it with their teeth.

[Verse 4 - George Washington: calm, cold, deep American voice, building to a slow finish]
You wanna talk chains? Your ships hauled more than any flag at sea.
Liverpool got rich while you stirred slave-grown sugar in your tea.
My will freed mine. Your empire kept its chains after you were dead,
so don't you preach me freedom with a crown upon your head.
I lost more battles than I won? Correct. I still won the war.
You won the fights and lost a continent. What were you fighting for?
The cherry tree's a myth? Fine. I cannot tell a lie, it's true:
I never chopped a cherry tree. I chopped your family tree in two.
Then your mind went. You talked for hours till you foamed,
so Doctor Willis strapped you in a chair. Your Majesty, enthroned.
Your piss ran blue. "Blue blood"? Nah, George. That was your bladder.
Herschel found a planet, and he named it "George's Star."
The world renamed it Uranus. That's exactly where you are.
In '82 you drafted your abdication, tried to give the crown away.
Same spring, they offered one to me. "Banish these thoughts." No way.
Then the war was won, and you asked your painter what I'd do.
West said, "He'll go back to his farm." And you (yes, you)
said, "If he does that, he'll be the greatest man in the world."
So I handed back my sword.
Then I handed back the presidency.
Your words, George. Not mine.
First thing you ever got right.
